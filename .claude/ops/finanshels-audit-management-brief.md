# finanshels.com — Search Visibility Audit
### Management Brief · 22 August 2026

**Scope:** Full technical audit of finanshels.com (727 URLs) across three disciplines — SEO (Google), AEO (featured snippets / answer boxes), GEO (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews). Every finding verified against the live site. Benchmarked against six UAE competitors.

---

## 1. Where we stand

| Discipline | Score | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **SEO** — traditional Google search | **38 / 100** | F |
| **AEO** — answer boxes & snippets | **23 / 100** | F |
| **GEO** — AI search engines | **25 / 100** | F |
| **Composite** | **30 / 100** | **F** |

**The headline: this is not a content problem. It is a plumbing problem — which is the good version of a bad score.**

We have the strongest raw asset base in our peer set: a 211-term glossary that **no competitor has**, 13 interactive calculators that **no competitor has**, 395 blog posts, 253 Trustpilot reviews at 4.9, and tier-1 press. We are #2 in the market by content volume.

Almost none of it is reachable by search engines, and almost none of it is machine-readable.

**The commercial consequence:** in 12 non-brand buying queries — "best accounting firms in Dubai", "bookkeeping services UAE", "VAT filing services Dubai" — **we appeared once.** In 6 local queries ("accountant near me Dubai", "VAT consultant Dubai") we appeared **zero times**, including in Sharjah, the emirate we are registered in.

---

## 2. What is broken

**① We are paying to produce content that cannot be found.**
Only **58 of 727 pages (8%)** are reachable within two clicks of the homepage. Five section hub pages — `/glossary`, `/industry`, `/team`, `/author`, `/topic` — return **404**, orphaning 249 pages. Blog pagination shows 12 posts at a time, putting older articles ~33 clicks deep.

**② 22 pages redirect into a dead end — and they are our best pages.**
Fifteen of our highest-value tax glossary terms — `qualifying-income`, `small-business-relief`, `input-vat`, `vat-return`, `federal-tax-authority-fta` — now redirect twice and land on a **404**. All fifteen are still submitted to Google in our sitemap. Meanwhile our current calculator pages are **missing** from the sitemap while seven obsolete duplicate versions are submitted in their place.

**③ Our location data is wrong, and it is costing us the Dubai market.**
Our website's structured data places the business at GPS coordinates that resolve to **a parking lot in the Sharjah desert — 56 km from our actual Dubai office.** We publish **four different phone numbers** and **three different addresses** across our own properties. Google validates local businesses by cross-referencing exactly these signals. Our own data prevents it.

**④ We have the best reviews in the market and search engines cannot see them.**
253 Trustpilot reviews at 4.9, growing ~165 in seven months. Zero rating markup on the site. Competitors with weaker reputations publish theirs and win the star ratings in search results. We are also **absent from Clutch and The Manifest** — directories that outrank every individual firm on "bookkeeping services Dubai".

**⑤ We are invisible to AI search, and competitors are describing us instead.**
No technical barrier exists — all 12 AI crawlers can read our site fine. The problem is we give them nothing structured to quote. We have **42 hand-written FAQ pairs and zero FAQ markup**. Asked to compare us to competitors, an AI engine produced an accurate profile of our pricing and what our plans exclude — sourced **entirely from a competitor's directory**, not from us.

**⑥ Our published claims contradict each other.**
We state client numbers as **5,000+, 6,000+ and 7,000+** on three different pages. Our homepage advertises **"From AED 804/month"** — a price that appears nowhere on our site; the pricing page says *"Pick the plan. We'll quote the number."* Our own articles disagree on the corporate tax record-retention period (5 vs 7 years), on late-filing penalties (AED 10,000 vs AED 500/month), and on when UAE corporate tax began. **All 395 blog posts are stamped "Updated On: August 2026"** — an automated timestamp, not a real review; some still cite deadlines from 2024.

**⑦ Our commercial pages are the thinnest in the market.**
Our main bookkeeping page is **757 words**. Competitors range 1,504–3,970. Our corporate tax page is 929 words against a competitor's 5,290. We have **no service pages at all** for excise tax, internal audit, tax residency certificates, payroll, or transfer pricing — categories where competitors run 27–46 pages each.

## 3. What needs to be done

Sequenced so that cheap, high-impact work lands first. Effort is engineering/content days, estimated.

| # | Action | Impact |

## 4. Decisions we need from management

These block the work. Fixing citations before answering them would propagate wrong data at scale.

| # | Decision | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Is our HQ Dubai or Sharjah?** Our website says Sharjah; LinkedIn, Trustpilot, Facebook, Apple and Bing say Dubai | Every address, map listing and location page depends on this |
| **2** | **Are we LLC, FZ LLC, or FZ-LLC?** All three appear in public sources | Legal-entity consistency is a trust signal for Google and clients |
| **3** | **Which tax figures are correct?** We publish conflicting retention periods and penalty amounts | We are publishing incorrect tax guidance to clients today |
| **4** | **Can we substantiate "FTA-approved tax agent"?** Marketed on tax.finanshels.com; we could not verify it against the FTA register | Regulatory-claim exposure under Google Ads policy |
| **5** | **Approve Search Console + analytics access** | See below — this is the biggest gap in this report |

## 5. What we could not measure

**We have no first-party performance data.** No Google Search Console connection, and the analytics project linked for this audit was the wrong one. That means:

- **No traffic, ranking-position, or impression data** for any page.
- **No Google review count or rating** — the single strongest local ranking factor.
- **No verified page-speed data.**

**Consequence:** the plan above is prioritised by **structural severity, not by revenue at risk.** We are fixing what is provably broken, in the order that unblocks the most — but we cannot yet say which fix earns the most money, and we will not be able to prove the impact of any of it.

**Recommendation:** grant Search Console and analytics access before Wave 1 begins. It costs nothing, takes an hour, and converts this from a defect list into a measurable business case.

---

**Bottom line for the board:** we scored 30/100, and every one of the top ten defects is a configuration or markup error — not a market disadvantage. The assets competitors cannot match, we already own. Waves 1 and 2 are roughly 23 days of work and address every critical defect. The honest caveat is that we cannot currently measure the return, and that is the first thing to fix.
